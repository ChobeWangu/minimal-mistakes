SYNTHETIC CHEMISTRY · PROTEIN ENGINEERING · BIOCONJUGATION

Building artificial metalloenzymes for sustainable catalysis

My Cambridge PhD research focused on developing synthetic porphyrin–protein conjugates as artificial metalloenzymes for sustainable and selective catalysis. Across 3.5 years, I combined metal–porphyrin cofactor design, synthesis and characterisation with protein modification, expression and bioconjugation. My thesis is now submitted, with the viva pending.

Synthetic organic chemistry · Mass spectrometry · Protein expression.
By integrating synthetic and biological chemistry, I aim to enable challenging transformations under mild, biologically compatible conditions and develop biomolecular catalysts with transformative potential.

DOCTORAL RESEARCH · 2022–2026

How an artificial metalloenzyme comes together

I combine engineered cytochrome b562 with synthetic Ir(III) and Zn(II) porphyrins. The protein creates a defined pocket around the metal cofactor, allowing us to investigate how a biological environment can shape non-natural reactivity.
